5 Air Conditioning Myths Costing You Money This July

Mid July is when the AC in your NW Pennsylvania home stops being a comfort item and starts being the thing standing between a normal day and a miserable one. It is also the point in the summer when the small beliefs people carry about how air conditioning works start costing real money, or worse, start affecting someone's health.

Some of these ideas get passed down for years without anyone checking them. Here are five of the most common ones I hear from homeowners around Venango, Crawford, and Warren counties, and what is actually true.

Myth: A Lower Thermostat Setting Cools the House Faster

This is the one almost everyone believes. A central air system runs at one speed. It is either on or off. Setting the thermostat to 65 instead of 72 does not make the unit push harder or cool faster. It just means the system keeps running past 72 on its way down to 65, using more electricity along the way.

Think of it like a car. Flooring the gas pedal does not make the engine bigger, it just burns more fuel while the car does the same job. If you walk into a hot house, setting the thermostat to the temperature you actually want gets you there just as fast as setting it lower, and it costs less to do it.

Myth: Leaving the AC Running All Day Is More Efficient

The logic sounds reasonable. Cooling a house that has heated up all day seems like it should take more energy than just holding a steady temperature the whole time you are gone. In most homes, it is the other way around. A house that is allowed to warm up while you are at work and then gets cooled back down when you return typically uses less total energy than running the AC nonstop.

There are exceptions. Homes with poor insulation that heat up extremely fast, or very humid conditions where the AC needs extra time to pull moisture back out of the air, can change the math. But for most NW PA homes, a programmable thermostat that lets the temperature rise a bit during the day and drops again shortly before you get home is the more efficient approach.

Myth: Air Conditioning Makes You Sick

Colds and other respiratory illnesses come from viruses, not from cold air. Sitting in an air conditioned room does not give you a virus any more than standing outside in winter does. What AC can do is dry out the air, and dry air can irritate your nose and throat enough to feel like the start of a cold when it is not one.

A dirty air filter makes this worse by circulating dust and allergens that trigger reactions people mistake for getting sick. The fix is simple. Change your filter regularly, and add a humidifier if the dryness bothers you. That is a very different problem than the AC actually making you ill.

Myth: A Fan Works Just as Well as AC in Extreme Heat

A fan cools you by helping sweat evaporate off your skin, which only works if the air moving across you is cooler than your body. Once the outdoor temperature climbs above the mid nineties, close to body temperature, a fan is just pushing hot air around and can make you feel worse, not better.

Fans are still worth running alongside your AC on normal hot days. A ceiling fan lets you set the thermostat a few degrees higher while feeling just as comfortable, which saves real money over a summer. Just do not count on a fan alone to protect you or a family member on the hottest days we get here in NW PA.

Myth: Closing Vents in Unused Rooms Saves Energy

It feels logical. Why cool a room nobody is using. But central AC systems are built and balanced for a set number of open vents. Closing some of them raises the pressure in the ductwork, which can force cooled air out through duct leaks, strain the blower motor, and even freeze the coil. None of that saves money, and some of it leads to a repair bill.

A better approach is to leave interior doors open so air can move through the house naturally, and let a programmable thermostat handle the savings by adjusting the temperature during the hours nobody is home.

What Actually Helps

The truth behind most of these myths comes down to the same idea. Your AC handles the heat that gets into your home. How much heat it has to fight depends on your insulation, your windows, and how well the house is sealed up. Improving those things, paired with a system that is properly sized and well maintained, does more for your comfort and your bill than chasing any thermostat trick.
